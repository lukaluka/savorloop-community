# Feedback and personalization

[Guide index](../README.md) · [Plans and meals](plans-and-meals.md)

SavorLoop learns from saved meal ratings and behavior signals. It uses those signals to help choose among eligible meals in later plans. A single positive rating does not promise that the same meal will appear next week, and repeated preferences cannot override a firm exclusion.

## Rate a meal

1. Open a meal from **Today** or **Plan**.
2. Choose **Rate meal**. **I ate this** also records that behavior and opens the rating screen.
3. If useful, select reasons under **Any reason? Optional** before choosing the rating.
4. Choose the rating that reflects your experience. The rating action saves and dismisses the screen.

Use the displayed rating choices rather than treating every negative experience as a permanent exclusion. Optional reasons can distinguish why the meal did not work for you without needing a public support ticket.

The current ratings are **Loved it**, **Liked it**, **Okay**, **Didn't like it**, and **Never again**. Optional reasons include too much work, too expensive, too bland, too repetitive, too much food, not enough food, and a schedule mismatch. Pick any reasons before tapping the rating, because choosing the rating saves it immediately.

## Understand “Never again”

**Never again** is a firm exclusion of that recipe. It keeps the recipe out of future recommendations until you explicitly allow it again. If the recipe appears in the active week, that week may be deactivated and need rebuilding. Earlier saved plan records remain in local history.

If the plan disappears immediately after this rating, that can be the expected response to the new exclusion. Return to **Today** or **Plan** and create a fresh plan. The app should not keep recommending the excluded recipe just to preserve the earlier week.

## Allow an excluded meal again

1. Open **You**.
2. Find **What SavorLoop is learning**.
3. Locate the recipe marked **Excluded from future plans**.
4. Tap **Allow this meal again**.
5. Rebuild or create a subsequent plan when you want the change considered.

Allowing a recipe again makes it eligible for consideration; it does not guarantee selection. Your allergies, ingredient exclusions, dietary restrictions, and other firm constraints still apply.

## Record what happened

On the meal screen, **I ate this** records an “Ate” signal. The **More** menu includes **Skipped** and **Ate out**. These are behavior signals associated with the meal. They do not capture the nutrients of an unlisted restaurant meal or verify everything consumed that day.

Feedback is saved as history. Reversing an exclusion changes the current preference state while keeping earlier events available in the local data history. The current app does not provide a general-purpose editor for rewriting every historical event.

## Check what the app learned

In **You**, the learning section shows saved meal ratings and visible preference signals. You may see explanations such as leaning toward a repeat, less often next time, or still learning. Treat those as a summary of observed preferences, not as a promise of a particular future menu.

If an excluded recipe returns, verify whether you explicitly allowed it again and whether you are viewing historical or current plan content. If the active recommendation still appears wrong, report the recipe name, the app build, and a fictional sequence of actions. Do not attach your full feedback export.
